Under Article 6 of the Associate Professorship Regulation, the application files of candidates are first assessed by the associate professorship evaluation jury as to whether there is any breach of scientific research and publication ethics and whether the minimum application requirements are met. Research and publication ethics issues relating to every kind of scientific research and work carried out by the candidate, scientific activities undertaken, and scientific research and development projects supported and/or conducted by the candidate are included in this review.

## What Are the Acts Contrary to Scientific Research and Publication Ethics?

Under Article 4 of the Inter-University Council Scientific Research and Publication Ethics Directive, the acts contrary to scientific research and publication ethics are as follows:

- **Plagiarism:** Presenting, in whole or in part, the original ideas, methods, data or works of others as one's own without citing them in accordance with scientific rules,
- **Fabrication:** Using data that does not actually exist or has been falsified in scientific research,
- **Distortion:** Falsifying research records or the data obtained, presenting devices or materials not used in the research as if they had been used, falsifying or shaping research results in line with the interests of the persons and organisations from which support was received,
- **Duplicate publication:** Presenting duplicate publications as separate publications in academic appointments and promotions,
- **Salami slicing:** Dividing the results of a research project into parts in an inappropriate manner that undermines the integrity of the research and publishing them in multiple pieces, and presenting these as separate publications in academic appointments and promotions,
- **Unjust authorship:** Including among the authors persons who have made no active contribution or failing to include those who have, changing the order of authors without justification and in an inappropriate manner, removing from later editions of the work the names of those who made an active contribution, having one's name included among the authors by using one's influence although one has made no active contribution,
- Failing to state the persons, institutions or organisations that provided support, and their contributions, in publications resulting from research conducted with support,
- Using, as a source and without the owner's permission, theses or works that have not yet been submitted or defended and accepted,
- Failing to respect patient rights in one's publications,
- Acting contrary to the relevant legislation in biomedical research involving humans or in other clinical research,
- Sharing with others, before publication and without the express permission of the author, information contained in a work one was assigned to review,
- Using for purposes other than intended the resources, spaces, facilities and devices provided or allocated for scientific research,
- Publishing data obtained in surveys and attitude studies carried out within a scientific work without obtaining the express consent of the participants or, where the research is to be conducted at an institution, without also obtaining the institution's permission,
- In research and experiments: harming animals and the ecological balance, failing to obtain in writing from the competent bodies the permits required before work begins, carrying out work contrary to the provisions of legislation or of the international conventions to which Türkiye is a party concerning the relevant research and experiments,
- Failing, on the part of researchers or officials, to comply with the obligation to inform and warn those concerned about potentially harmful practices relating to the scientific research carried out,
- In scientific work, failing to use data and information obtained from other persons and institutions to the extent and in the manner permitted, failing to respect the confidentiality of such information and failing to ensure its protection,
- Making false or misleading statements about scientific research and publications in applications for academic appointments and promotions.
- Behaving, contrary to academic conventions, in a manner ethically unexpected of an associate professorship candidate.
- Publishing by presenting in the application file a journal that does not exist as existing, or a journal that is not indexed as indexed, or a non-refereed journal as refereed.

## What Cannot Be Considered Contrary to Scientific Research and Publication Ethics?

The use in works of propositions such as universally recognised scientific theories, the basic knowledge of scientific fields, and mathematical theorems and proofs cannot be considered an ethics violation.

## Are Associate Professorship Candidates Obliged to State Earlier Ethics Violation Decisions in New Applications?

Associate professorship candidates must list in their curriculum vitae and list of works all of their works relating to academic studies. Candidates who applied for associate professorship in earlier periods and were found to have committed an ethics violation must indicate those publications in the publication list with a brief explanation.

## How Is an Ethics Violation Decision Reviewed by the Scientific Research and Publication Ethics Commission?

Ethics violation allegations made to the Inter-University Council Presidency are forwarded to the relevant commission as soon as possible. The commission first determines whether the allegations are based on credible information and documents. Persons about whom a review has been started on the basis of an ethics violation allegation are asked for a written defence on the allegations, together with the necessary information and documents. The persons concerned must submit their defence within fifteen days. If no defence is submitted within this period, the Commission conducts its review on the basis of the information and documents available. Where deemed necessary, the defence may also be taken orally.

All correspondence with other institutions and organisations during the commissions' review process is carried out through the Inter-University Council Presidency. Decisions to be taken are voted on after being opened to discussion in the Commissions and are signed by the Commission members. A member who dissents from the decision must write the reasons for the dissenting vote. The decisions taken are notified to the President of the Inter-University Council. The President carries out what the decisions require. Commission members and experts/expert witnesses may not disclose any information or document relating to their review without the permission of the Inter-University Council Presidency. The Commissions are entirely independent in their activities and review and decide applications in terms of compliance with ethical principles.

## What Actions Are Taken as a Result of the Ethics Review?

As a result of the review of an allegation of breach of scientific research and publication ethics, the associate professorship application of a candidate found to have committed an ethics violation is cancelled. A candidate whose application has been cancelled may reapply, counting from the application period, at the earliest in the third following period. Provisions on the candidate's administrative, criminal and legal liability are reserved.

In respect of persons found to have committed acts contrary to scientific research and publication ethics, the matter is reported to the Council of Higher Education Presidency so that the necessary disciplinary and other administrative, legal and criminal proceedings may be carried out under the relevant legislation.

Where acts contrary to ethics have occurred, the consent of the persons and organisations harmed or whose rights have been adversely affected does not remove the application of an ethical sanction against those concerned.

## What Are the Limitation Periods for Ethics Violations?

The starting of a review for ethics violations in the form of plagiarism, fabrication and distortion is not subject to any time limit. For ethics violations other than these, an ethics review cannot be conducted once ten years have passed from the date on which the act took place.

For ethics violations other than plagiarism, fabrication and distortion, an ethics violation allegation cannot be reviewed if an ethics review is not started within two years from the date on which the act constituting the ethics violation became known.

If the work is republished in whole or in part and used in academic appointments and promotions, the limitation periods stated above start to run anew.

## Is a Re-Review Possible Regarding an Ethics Violation?

In respect of the works of associate professorship candidates about whom a review has been started on the basis of an ethics violation allegation, reviews and investigations carried out or ongoing by other institutions and organisations or by the ethics boards of higher education institutions, even if they relate to the same allegations, do not constitute an obstacle to ethics violation reviews to be conducted under this regulation.

No application or review may be made on a matter that has been reviewed and decided by the Scientific Research and Ethics Commission, unless it is on the same allegations and/or new evidence is shown.

## Are the Decisions of the Scientific Research and Publication Ethics Commission Confidential?

The decisions of the Scientific Research and Publication Ethics Commissions are confidential, apart from the general regulations. The decisions taken as a result of reviews conducted under this regulation are filed and kept appropriately by the Inter-University Council Presidency, in compliance with the principle of confidentiality.
