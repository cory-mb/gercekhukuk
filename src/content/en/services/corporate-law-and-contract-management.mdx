## Scope

A company's legal needs start with incorporation and multiply as it grows and its shareholding changes. The work on this page falls into two strands: matters concerning the company's own structure, and the contracts it enters with third parties.

## Corporate law

- Incorporation of joint stock and limited companies; articles of association
- Shareholders' agreements: management, voting, transfer restrictions, exit provisions
- Share transfers and capital increases/decreases
- Preparing general assembly and board resolutions
- Actions to annul or declare void general assembly resolutions
- Withdrawal and expulsion of shareholders
- Mergers, demergers and conversions
- Unfair competition claims and interim injunctions

Many shareholder disputes stem from rules that were never written down at incorporation. Addressing in the shareholders' agreement what happens if a shareholder wants to leave, dies or sets up a competing business makes most later litigation unnecessary.

## Contract management

Contract management does not end with drafting. If a company's contracts sit in scattered folders, renewal dates are missed, penalty clauses go unnoticed and it becomes unclear who is authorised to sign what.

### Drafting and review

- Sale, supply, service, dealership, distribution and franchise agreements
- Non-disclosure and non-compete agreements
- Licence and software agreements
- Risk review of contracts drafted by the other side, with suggested revisions
- Support during negotiations; addenda

### After signature

- Standard contract templates for the company
- Approval and signing authority workflow
- Tracking of term, renewal and notice dates
- Periodic review of the contract portfolio

In businesses without an in-house legal team, this tracking is often nobody's job. Even a simple contract register with a named owner makes the risks visible.

## Commercial disputes

- Claims for receivables and damages under contracts
- Penalty clause disputes
- Disputes over cheques and promissory notes ([Enforcement Law](/en/services/enforcement-law))
- Mandatory commercial mediation

Turkish law expects merchants to act as prudent businesspeople. A merchant cannot, for example, ask for an agreed penalty to be reduced as excessive. In commercial contracts, review before signature is more effective than anything done afterwards.
