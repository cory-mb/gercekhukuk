faqs:
  - q: "Should I set up a limited liability company or a joint stock company?"
    a: "The choice of company type depends on the capital structure, the number of shareholders, the transferability of shares and the liability regime of the shareholders. The minimum capital is TL 250,000 for joint stock companies and TL 50,000 for limited liability companies. Both types can be established by natural or legal persons and by a single shareholder; however, a limited liability company cannot have more than fifty shareholders, whereas a joint stock company has no such limit. The transfer of shares is subject to a more flexible regime in a joint stock company than in a limited liability company. In both joint stock and limited liability companies, shareholders as a rule have no direct liability for the company's debts. Their obligations arising from their status as shareholders consist of paying the capital they have undertaken and, if provided for in the articles of association, performing ancillary obligations; in limited liability companies, an additional payment obligation may also be imposed. Only joint stock companies can be listed on the stock exchange. The choice should be made by assessing all of these factors together."
  - q: "Who can bring an action to annul general assembly resolutions, and within what period?"
    a: "An annulment action may be brought before the commercial court of first instance at the place of the company's registered office, within three months from the date of the resolution, against general assembly resolutions that violate the law or the articles of association, and in particular the principle of good faith (TCC Art. 445). Under TCC Art. 446, those who may bring this action are:\n• Shareholders who were present at the meeting, voted against the resolution and had their dissent recorded in the minutes,\n• Shareholders, whether or not they were present at the meeting and whether or not they voted, who allege that the invitation was not properly made, that the agenda was not properly announced, that persons not entitled to attend the general assembly attended and voted, or that they themselves were wrongfully not permitted to attend and vote, and that these violations affected the adoption of the resolution,\n• The board of directors,\n• Each member of the board of directors, if the implementation of the resolutions would give rise to their personal liability."
  - q: "Is mediation mandatory in commercial disputes?"
    a: "Yes. Among the commercial lawsuits specified in Article 4 of the Turkish Commercial Code and in other laws, in receivable, compensation, annulment of objection, negative declaratory and restitution actions whose subject matter is a sum of money, applying to a mediator before filing the lawsuit is a procedural requirement (TCC Art. 5/A). The mediator concludes the application within six weeks from the date of appointment; this period may be extended by up to two weeks in cases of necessity."
  - q: "Are shareholders of joint stock and limited liability companies liable for the company's public debts?"
    a: "Shareholders of a limited liability company are directly liable, in proportion to their capital shares, for public receivables that cannot be collected from the company in whole or in part, or that are found to be uncollectible (Law No. 6183, Art. 35). A shareholder who transfers their share remains jointly and severally liable with the transferee for public debts relating to the period before the transfer. In a joint stock company, on the other hand, shareholders are liable only up to the capital share they have undertaken and only towards the company (TCC Art. 329); they are not held liable for the company's public debts by reason of being shareholders. In both types of company, legal representatives such as members of the board of directors and managers may be held jointly and severally liable for the whole of the public receivable (Law No. 6183, Repeated Art. 35)."
  - q: "What is an ordinary partnership and how are the partners liable?"
    a: "An ordinary partnership is a contract by which two or more persons undertake to combine their labour and assets in order to achieve a common purpose (TCO Art. 620). An ordinary partnership has no legal personality. Unless otherwise agreed in the partnership agreement, the partners' contributions must be of the importance and nature required by the purpose of the partnership and equal to one another. Unless otherwise agreed, the partners are jointly and severally liable towards third parties, acting together or through a representative, for the debts they have undertaken within the framework of the partnership relationship."

## Company Law

Company law covers all stages of a company from its incorporation to its liquidation. The allocation of authority between the company's organs, the rights of shareholders and the duties of care and loyalty of the management body are decisive at every stage, from the company's day-to-day operation to changes in its ownership structure. Organ resolutions that have not been properly adopted, gaps in the share ledger or uncertainty about the authority to represent the company are often noticed only when a share transfer, a capital increase, an investment negotiation or a dispute between shareholders arises. Advisory and representation services in this field cover ensuring that company transactions are carried out in accordance with the legislation and the articles of association, establishing a sound contractual basis for commercial relationships, and resolving any disputes that may arise within a legal framework. The services we provide in this scope are as follows:

### Relating to incorporation and ownership structure

- Incorporation of joint stock and limited liability companies, preparation and amendment of articles of association
- Shareholders' agreements: management, voting, transfer restrictions, exit provisions
- Applications and registration procedures before the trade registry directorate

### Relating to corporate governance

- Preparation of general assembly and board of directors meetings and resolutions
- General assembly internal directive, board of directors internal directive and delegation of authority arrangements; arrangements on signature authority, representation and binding the company
- Legal framework for the share ledger and other company books
- Legal review of transactions such as profit distribution, reserves and shareholders' indebtedness to the company

### Relating to share transactions and restructuring

- Share transfers, capital increases and reductions
- Legal due diligence in share and company acquisitions, investment and exit processes
- Mergers, demergers and change of company type
- Liquidation procedures

### Relating to shareholder disputes

- Actions to annul general assembly resolutions and to establish their nullity
- Actions for withdrawal and expulsion of a shareholder in limited liability companies; actions for dissolution for just cause in joint stock companies
- Actions concerning the liability of members of the board of directors and managers

### Relating to commercial disputes

- Receivable and compensation actions arising from contracts
- Actions arising from intellectual property rights and unfair competition
- Disputes arising from cheques and promissory notes ([Enforcement Law](/en/services/enforcement-law))
- Representing the company in mandatory commercial mediation
