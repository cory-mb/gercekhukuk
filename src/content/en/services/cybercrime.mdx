## Why cybercrime needs particular care

In cybercrime cases evidence disappears quickly: a log is deleted, an account is closed, a message is unsent. The right steps in the first hours often determine the outcome.

Av. Kazım Onur Gerçek completed a master's degree in forensic informatics at Gazi University in 2015. How digital evidence is obtained, preserved and interpreted is an integral part of the legal analysis in these files.

## Offences against information systems under the Turkish Criminal Code

- **Unauthorised access (Art. 243):** Unlawfully entering or remaining in an information system.
- **Hindering, disrupting, deleting or altering data (Art. 244):** Disrupting a system or deleting, altering or making data inaccessible.
- **Misuse of bank or credit cards (Art. 245):** Using another person's card or a counterfeit card.
- **Prohibited devices or software (Art. 245/A):** Producing, selling or possessing devices or programs for these offences.

## Other offences committed through information systems

- Aggravated fraud using information systems
- Unlawful recording, disclosure or acquisition of personal data
- Violation of privacy and of confidentiality of communications
- Insult, threats and blackmail via social media
- Intellectual property infringements

## For victims

- Identifying and preserving evidence
- Drafting the criminal complaint
- Following the investigation and prosecution
- Requests for blocking access and removal of content
- Claims for material and moral damages

## For suspects and defendants

- Defence at statement and interrogation stages
- Reviewing the lawfulness of search and seizure
- Technical and legal assessment of digital evidence
- Defence at trial

## For organisations

Unauthorised access to a company's systems can also be a personal data breach. Alongside the criminal process, notification to the Personal Data Protection Board within 72 hours may be required.
