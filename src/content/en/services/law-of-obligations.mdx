## What the law of obligations covers

When you buy a service, rent a home or suffer loss in an accident, the law of obligations applies. Turkish Code of Obligations No. 6098 governs the formation and performance of contracts, liability in tort and unjust enrichment.

Most disputes come down to three questions: Did an obligation arise, was it performed, and if not, what follows?

## Contractual disputes

- Claims for payment, performance and damages
- Rescission and termination
- Defective goods and services
- Contracts for work (construction, renovation, software development)
- Agency and service contracts
- Adaptation of contracts for excessive hardship

In sales, the buyer must examine the goods after delivery as is customary and notify defects within a reasonable time. Late notice can weaken even a valid claim.

## Torts and damages

- Personal injury outside the employment context
- Material and moral damages claims
- Infringement of personality rights
- Restitution for unjust enrichment

Limitation periods in damages claims can be short: two years from learning of the damage and the liable person.

## Lease disputes

Leases generate more litigation than any other area of the law of obligations. Residential and roofed commercial leases are subject to special tenant-protection rules.

### Rent increases and rent determination

- Whether the annual increase complies with the legal cap
- Rent determination actions in tenancies over five years
- Adaptation of rent

Use the [rent increase calculator](/en/calculators/rent-increase-rate) to check the rate.

### Eviction cases

- Need of the landlord or relatives for housing or business premises
- Reconstruction and major renovation
- Written undertaking to vacate
- Two justified written warnings within one lease year
- Default in paying rent
- Expiry of the ten-year extension period

Since 1 September 2023, mediation is mandatory before suing in lease disputes. For collecting rent and eviction through enforcement, see [Enforcement Law](/en/services/enforcement-law).

## Limitation periods

The general limitation period is ten years. A five-year period applies to rent, interest and certain service claims. Which period applies depends on the nature of the claim and directly shapes strategy.
