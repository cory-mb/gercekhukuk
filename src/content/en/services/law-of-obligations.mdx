faqs:
  - q: "What rights are available if the other party does not perform the contract?"
    a: "In contracts that impose obligations on both parties, if the debtor falls into default, the creditor may grant the debtor an appropriate additional period to perform. If the obligation is still not performed within that period, the creditor may demand performance and compensation for delay; waive performance and claim compensation for the loss arising from non-performance (positive interest); or withdraw from the contract, reclaim what was given and claim compensation for the loss arising from the contract becoming void (negative interest) (TCO Arts. 123–125)."
  - q: "What is the difference between withdrawal from a contract and termination?"
    a: "Withdrawal (rescission) removes the contract retroactively, as if it had been void from the moment it was formed. The parties then demand back what they have given each other; if they have not yet performed, their obligations to perform end. Termination ends the contract for the future only."
  - q: "What are the buyer's rights when buying defective goods, and when must the defect be notified?"
    a: "After taking delivery, the buyer must inspect the goods as soon as the ordinary course of business allows and notify the seller of any defect within a reasonable time; a buyer who gives no notice is deemed to have accepted the goods (TCO Art. 223). In sales between merchants, an obvious defect must be notified within two days and a non-obvious defect within eight days (Turkish Commercial Code Art. 23). A buyer who gives timely notice may choose one of these rights: withdrawing from the contract, a price reduction, free repair, or replacement with a non-defective equivalent, and may claim damages if the conditions are met (TCO Art. 227)."
  - q: "When do claims become time-barred?"
    a: "Unless the law provides otherwise, every claim is subject to a ten-year limitation period (TCO Art. 146). Periodic performances such as rent, interest on principal and wages, and claims arising from agency, commission, commercial agency and contracts for work, are subject to a five-year period; however, the five-year period does not apply to claims arising from a contract for work where the contractor failed to perform, or performed improperly, through gross fault (TCO Art. 147). Claims arising from tort and unjust enrichment are time-barred two years after the claimant learns of them and in any event ten years after the act was committed or the enrichment occurred (TCO Arts. 72, 82). If the tort also constitutes a crime for which criminal law provides a longer limitation period, that longer period applies."
  - q: "Under what conditions is a surety contract valid?"
    a: "A surety contract is not valid unless it is made in writing and states the maximum amount for which the surety is liable and the date of the contract. The surety must write in their own hand, in the contract, the maximum amount, the date, and, in the case of a joint and several surety, that they undertake the obligation in that capacity or with words to that effect (TCO Art. 583). For a married person to act as surety, except for exceptions in the law, the spouse's written consent, given before the contract is made or at the latest when it is made, is required (TCO Art. 584). The absence of any one of these conditions invalidates the suretyship."
  - q: "What is the maximum rent increase?"
    a: "Agreements between the parties on the rent for a renewed lease period are valid provided the increase does not exceed the change in the 12-month average consumer price index (CPI) for the previous lease year. If the parties cannot agree, the rent is set by the judge on an equitable basis, taking the condition of the premises into account and without exceeding the same limit (TCO Art. 344)."
  - q: "Can the landlord evict the tenant when the lease term ends?"
    a: "No. In residential and roofed commercial leases, the landlord cannot evict the tenant merely on the ground that the term has expired. Unless the tenant gives notice at least fifteen days before the end of a fixed-term lease, the lease is deemed extended for one year on the same terms. At the end of the ten-year extension period, the landlord may terminate the lease without giving a reason by giving notice at least three months before the end of any extension year. In indefinite-term leases, the tenant may terminate at any time, while the landlord may terminate by notice only after ten years have passed from the start of the lease (TCO Art. 347)."
  - q: "In what cases can a tenant be evicted?"
    a: "A tenant can be evicted only if one of the grounds listed in the law exists. The law divides these grounds into those arising from the landlord and those arising from the tenant.\n\nGrounds arising from the landlord: The need of the landlord, their spouse, descendants, ascendants or persons they are legally obliged to support for a residence or workplace, and substantial repair, extension or alteration of the premises for the purpose of reconstruction or redevelopment (TCO Art. 350), and the need of a new owner who later acquired the premises (TCO Art. 351).\n\nGrounds arising from the tenant: A written undertaking to vacate, giving rise to two justified notices for non-payment of rent within one lease year, and the tenant or their spouse living with them owning a residence suitable for living in within the municipal boundaries of the same district or town (TCO Art. 352)."
  - q: "What are the validity conditions of a written undertaking to vacate?"
    a: "For an undertaking to vacate to be valid, it must be in writing, state a specific vacating date, be signed by the tenant or an authorised representative, clearly describe the premises, and be given after the premises were handed over to the tenant. Undertakings signed together with or before the lease are, as a rule, invalid. If there is more than one tenant, the undertaking must be given by all tenants. Another frequent question in practice is whether an undertaking to vacate with a blank date is valid. In a dispute before the Grand Chamber of Civil Chambers of the Court of Cassation, the court did not treat the giving of a blank-dated undertaking as invalid outright; it held that the burden of proving the undertaking's invalidity lies on the tenant. (Court of Cassation Grand Chamber of Civil Chambers, 28.09.2021, 2017/975 E., 2021/1108 K.)."
  - q: "When should a rent determination lawsuit be filed?"
    a: "A rent determination lawsuit can be filed at any time. However, if it is filed at least thirty days before the start of the new rent period, or if the landlord gives the tenant a written notice of increase within that period and files the lawsuit by the end of the new period, the rent set by the court applies from the start of the new period. If the lease contains a rent increase clause, the same result follows for a lawsuit filed by the end of the new period (TCO Art. 345)."
  - q: "Is the deposit returned at its original amount or at its current value?"
    a: "In residential and roofed commercial leases, the deposit cannot exceed three months' rent. A deposit given in cash is placed in a term account at a bank, to be withdrawn only with the landlord's consent. The bank releases the deposit with the consent of both parties or on the basis of a final enforcement proceeding or court decision. If the landlord does not notify the bank in writing within three months of the end of the lease that they have sued or started enforcement proceedings against the tenant, the bank releases the deposit at the tenant's request (TCO Art. 342).\n\nA frequent problem in practice is how a deposit that was not placed in a term account at a bank is to be returned. The law does not regulate whether it is returned at the amount originally given or in an updated amount. Court of Cassation decisions take different approaches: return at the original amount, return with interest, and return after adjusting the initial deposit-to-rent ratio to the last rent. It is therefore useful to regulate in the lease how the deposit will be returned."

The law of obligations is the branch of law that governs debt relationships between persons. Disputes arising from obligations based on contract, tort and unjust enrichment, and from specific contracts such as sale, lease, contract for work, agency, gift and loan, fall within its scope. Our advisory and litigation services in the law of obligations mainly cover the following:

- Drafting, reviewing and negotiating contracts
- Lawsuits for receivables, performance and damages
- Debtor's default; granting additional time, withdrawal from and termination of contracts
- Lawsuits for material and non-material damages arising from tort
- Bodily injury claims arising from traffic accidents and other events, and loss-of-support compensation claims (excluding work accidents)
- Non-material damages claims arising from violations of personality rights
- Disputes arising from unjust enrichment
- Disputes arising from defective goods and liability for defects
- Disputes arising from specific contracts such as contracts for work, services and leases
- Disputes arising from ordinary partnership agreements

## Lease Law

Residential and roofed commercial leases are regulated in the Turkish Code of Obligations by special provisions that protect the tenant (TCO Art. 339 et seq.). Because a rent increase and the termination of the lease are possible only within the limits and on the grounds set by law, compliance with the periods for notices, notifications and filing lawsuits is of great importance. Our advisory and litigation services in lease law are as follows:

### Disputes concerning the rent

- Reviewing whether the rent increase complies with the statutory limit
- Rent determination lawsuits
- Equitable re-determination of the rent in leases longer than five years
- Disputes concerning rent agreed in a foreign currency
- Adjustment of the rent due to excessive difficulty of performance

Use the [rent increase calculator](/en/calculators/rent-increase-rate) to check the rate.

### Disputes concerning eviction

- Eviction because of the need of the landlord, their spouse, descendants, ascendants or persons they are legally obliged to support for a residence or workplace
- Eviction because of the need of a new owner
- Eviction for substantial repair, extension or alteration of the premises for the purpose of reconstruction or redevelopment
- Eviction based on an undertaking to vacate
- Eviction for giving rise to two justified notices within one lease year
- Eviction for non-payment of rent (default)
- Eviction because the tenant or their spouse living with them owns a residence suitable for living in within the municipal boundaries of the same district or town
- Termination of the lease on expiry of the ten-year extension period

### Other lease disputes

- Return of the deposit
- The tenant's rights and rent reduction because of defects in the premises
- Transfer of the lease and sub-letting in commercial leases
- Drafting and reviewing lease agreements
- Compensation claims for breach of the re-letting prohibition

In lease disputes, since 1 September 2023, applying to a mediator before filing a lawsuit is a procedural requirement, except for eviction through enforcement proceedings without a court judgment.
