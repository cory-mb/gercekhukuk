faqs:
  - q: "What are the conditions for severance pay?"
    a: "Severance pay requires that the employee has worked for at least one year, at the same or different workplaces of the same employer, and that the employment contract is terminated by the employee or the employer in a way that gives rise to severance pay."
  - q: "In which cases is severance pay payable?"
    a: "• Termination by the employer, other than for the immoral or bad-faith conduct and similar grounds set out in Article 25(II) of the Labour Law No. 4857,\n• Termination by the employee for the reasons set out in Article 24 of Law No. 4857,\n• Termination due to compulsory military service,\n• Termination by the employee in order to receive an old-age, retirement or disability pension, or a lump-sum payment, from the institution or fund to which the employee is affiliated by law,\n• Termination by a female employee of her own accord within one year of her marriage,\n• Termination of the employment contract due to the employee's death,\n• Resignation of an employee who has completed all retirement conditions other than age, namely the insurance period and the number of premium days."
  - q: "Can an employee who resigns receive severance pay?"
    a: "As a rule, no; an employee who resigns without giving a reason is not entitled to severance pay. However, an employee with at least one year of service who terminates the contract for one of the just causes listed in Article 24 of the Labour Law is entitled to it. Severance pay is also payable where retirement or old-age pension conditions are met, in cases of compulsory military service, and where a female employee leaves within one year of her marriage. Using the word “resignation” in the letter is not decisive on its own; however, since the employee is in principle bound by the reason given in the notice, it is important to state that reason clearly in the letter."
  - q: "How long are the notice periods?"
    a: "In indefinite-term employment contracts, the notice period depends on the employee's length of service: 2 weeks for less than six months, 4 weeks for six months to one and a half years, 6 weeks for one and a half to three years, and 8 weeks for more than three years. A party that terminates the contract without observing these periods pays the other party notice pay equal to the wages for the notice period."
  - q: "When can a reinstatement claim be filed?"
    a: "An application to a mediator must be made within one month of notification of the termination. If no agreement is reached in mediation, the case must be filed with the labour court within two weeks of the date of the final mediation report."
  - q: "Is mediation mandatory in employment disputes?"
    a: "Under the Labour Courts Law No. 7036, mediation is a precondition for suing in cases concerning employee or employer receivables and compensation based on individual or collective employment contracts, and reinstatement claims, as well as related objection, negative declaratory and restitution actions. It is not a precondition for material and moral damages arising from work accidents or occupational diseases, or for related declaratory, objection and recourse actions. We represent the employee or employer as party counsel in this process. See the Mediation page for details."
  - q: "What is mobbing, and what are the rights of an employee who is subjected to it?"
    a: "Mobbing is psychological harassment that targets a particular person in the workplace and is systematic, deliberate and continuous, aimed at intimidating and driving the person out of the job. Under Article 417 of the Turkish Code of Obligations No. 6098, the employer must take the necessary measures to prevent employees from being subjected to psychological and sexual harassment and to prevent further harm to those who have been. Depending on the circumstances, an employee subjected to mobbing may terminate the employment contract for just cause and claim severance pay, and, where the conditions are met, may also claim moral damages and discrimination compensation."
