  - q: "Do I have to reach a settlement in mediation?"
    a: "No. There is no obligation to settle, whether in mandatory or voluntary mediation. What is mandatory in mandatory mediation is applying to a mediator before filing a lawsuit; reaching a settlement is not required. If the parties cannot agree, a final report is issued and a lawsuit may be filed."
  - q: "Who pays the mediation fee?"
    a: "If the parties settle, the mediation fee is paid equally by the parties unless agreed otherwise. If no settlement is reached in mandatory mediation, the fee for two hours is paid from the budget of the Ministry of Justice; this amount is later included in the litigation costs of any subsequent lawsuit and is collected from the party that loses the case."
  - q: "How long does the mediation process take?"
    a: "The mediator concludes the process within six weeks from the date of appointment in commercial disputes, and within three weeks in employment, consumer, lease and other mandatory mediation disputes. In cases of necessity, these periods may be extended by up to two weeks in commercial disputes and by up to one week in the others."
  - q: "What happens if I do not attend the mediation meeting?"
    a: "Not attending the meeting does not close the path to court; the mediation ends and a final report is issued. However, a party that fails to attend the first meeting without a valid excuse is recorded in the final report and, even if it prevails in the subsequent lawsuit, bears half of the litigation costs. In addition, half of the attorney's fee, determined under the Attorneyship Minimum Fee Tariff, is awarded in favour of the other party. If neither party attends the first meeting, each party bears its own litigation costs."
  - q: "What happens if the settlement agreement is not complied with?"
    a: "Except in cases where a certificate of enforceability is required by law, a settlement agreement signed jointly by the parties, their lawyers and the mediator (and, in commercial disputes, by the lawyers and the mediator) is treated as a document having the nature of a court judgment, without requiring a certificate of enforceability. For settlement agreements that do not meet these conditions, the parties may request a certificate of enforceability from the civil court of peace at the place where the mediator operates, or, if mediation was sought during a lawsuit, from the court hearing the case. A settlement agreement bearing this certificate is also treated as a document having the nature of a court judgment, and enforcement proceedings based on a judgment may be started directly on it."
  - q: "When does the mediation process begin and how does it affect time limits?"
    a: "Where a mediator is approached before a lawsuit is filed, the process begins on the date on which the parties are invited to the first meeting, an agreement is reached between the parties and the mediator to continue the process, and this is recorded in a report. Where a mediator is approached after a lawsuit has been filed, the process begins on the date on which the parties accept the court's invitation to mediation, or on which they declare in writing to the court, outside a hearing, that they have agreed to apply to a mediator, or on which this declaration is recorded in the minutes of a hearing. The period from the beginning to the end of the mediation process is not taken into account in calculating limitation periods and forfeiture periods.\n\nIn mandatory mediation, limitation is suspended and forfeiture periods do not run on the matters in dispute from the application to the court-house mediation bureau until the date on which the final report is issued."

Mediation is a dispute resolution method conducted with the participation of an impartial and independent third person who has received specialist training. By applying systematic techniques, the mediator brings the parties together to talk and negotiate, establishes a process of communication between them so that they understand each other and thus produce their own solutions, and, where it becomes apparent that the parties cannot produce a solution, may also put forward a proposed solution (Law No. 6325, Art. 2). The mediator is the natural person who carries out the mediation activity and who is registered in the register of mediators kept by the Ministry of Justice.

As Gerçek Hukuk ve Arabuluculuk Bürosu, we act as mediator or as legal representative of a party in mandatory and voluntary mediation processes.

## What Mediation Offers

- **Time and cost:** In mandatory mediation, the process is concluded within the short periods set by law. If a settlement is reached, the length and expense of long court proceedings are avoided.
- **Confidentiality:** As a rule, information and documents submitted in the process are confidential. The request to start the process, proposals made during the meetings and matters accepted cannot be relied on as evidence in a lawsuit filed later.
- **Control stays with the parties:** Only the parties decide whether a settlement will be reached and what it will contain.
- **Interest-based solutions:** A dispute can be resolved not only according to legal claims but on the basis of the parties' needs and interests. In this respect, mediation is particularly suitable where commercial and personal relationships are to continue.
- **Enforceability:** Where the conditions set by law are met, the settlement agreement can be put into enforcement like a court judgment.

## Mandatory Mediation

In the following disputes, applying to a mediator before filing a lawsuit is mandatory. A lawsuit filed without applying to a mediator is dismissed on procedural grounds for lack of a procedural requirement.

- Lawsuits filed on the basis of an employee's or employer's receivable and compensation arising from law or from an individual or collective employment contract, and reinstatement claims, as well as actions for annulment of objection, negative declaratory actions and restitution actions relating to such receivables and compensation (claims for pecuniary and non-pecuniary damages arising from work accidents or occupational diseases, and related declaratory, objection and recourse actions, are not within the scope of mandatory mediation)
- Disputes heard before Consumer Courts (the exceptions listed in the sub-paragraphs of Art. 73/A(1) of the Consumer Protection Law are not within the scope of mandatory mediation)
- Among the commercial lawsuits specified in Article 4 of the Turkish Commercial Code and in other laws, receivable, compensation, annulment of objection, negative declaratory and restitution actions whose subject matter is a sum of money
- Disputes arising from lease relationships, except for the provisions on eviction of leased immovable property through enforcement proceedings without a judgment under Law No. 2004
- Disputes concerning the partition of movable and immovable property and the dissolution of co-ownership
- Disputes arising from the Condominium Law No. 634
- Disputes arising from neighbour rights
- Disputes arising from agricultural production contracts

## Voluntary Mediation

The parties may, of their own will, apply to a mediator before a lawsuit is filed or during a lawsuit, both in disputes within the scope of mandatory mediation and in private-law disputes outside it that arise from matters or transactions over which they may freely dispose. Article 17/B of Law No. 6325 also expressly provides that disputes concerning the transfer of immovable property or the creation of limited real rights over it are suitable for mediation.

## Which Disputes Are Not Suitable for Mediation?

Criminal cases, cases concerning the civil registry, matters over which the parties cannot freely dispose such as divorce and custody, disputes involving allegations of domestic violence, and disputes within the jurisdiction of the administrative courts are not suitable for mediation.

## How Does the Mediation Process Work?

1. **Application:** In mandatory mediation, the application is made to the mediation bureau at the place of the court competent to hear the merits of the dispute, and the mediator is appointed by the bureau from the list. In voluntary mediation, unless another procedure is agreed, the mediator or mediators are chosen jointly by the parties.
2. **Invitation and first meeting:** After being selected, the mediator invites the parties to the first meeting as soon as possible and informs them about how the process works, confidentiality and the legal consequences of a settlement.
3. **Negotiation:** The mediator makes efforts to enable the parties to set out their essential interests and needs and to reach an interest-based settlement accordingly. Where it becomes apparent that the parties cannot produce a solution, the mediator may propose an interest-based solution; however, the mediator cannot force the parties to accept any proposal.
4. **Outcome:** If a settlement is reached, a settlement agreement is drawn up and the parties cannot file a lawsuit on the matters agreed. If no settlement is reached, recourse may be had to court on the basis of the final report that is issued.

## The Role of Legal Representation in Mediation

The mediator is impartial and cannot give legal advice to either party. For this reason, it is useful for the parties to take part in the process with their own lawyers, so that they can correctly assess their rights and consciously determine the content of the settlement agreement. A person who acts as mediator in a dispute cannot be the lawyer of one of the parties in that dispute.
