## What is mediation?

Mediation is a process in which the parties try to resolve their dispute without going to court, with the help of an impartial and independent mediator. The mediator does not decide; they help the parties understand each other and reach their own solution.

## Mandatory mediation

In some disputes, applying to a mediator before suing is mandatory. A case filed without it is dismissed on procedural grounds.

## Voluntary mediation

Any dispute the parties can freely settle may go to mediation even where it is not mandatory. Shareholder disputes, long-term commercial relationships and family business conflicts are particularly suitable where the relationship is meant to continue.

## How it works

1. **Application:** In mandatory mediation, the application is made to the mediation office at the courthouse and a mediator is assigned from the list. In voluntary mediation, the parties choose the mediator together.
2. **First meeting:** The mediator explains the process and its consequences.
3. **Sessions:** Joint and, where useful, separate sessions are held.
4. **Final report:** If the parties settle, a settlement agreement is drawn up; otherwise the final report opens the way to court.

## Fees

Mediation fees follow the Mediation Minimum Fee Tariff published each year. Use the [mediation fee calculator](/en/calculators/mediation-fee) for an estimate.

## The mediator's role differs from a lawyer's

The mediator is impartial and does not give legal advice to either party. It is useful for parties to attend with their own lawyers so they can assess their rights properly. A person acting as mediator in a dispute cannot act as lawyer for either party in that dispute.

To attend mediation with a lawyer, see [Employment Law](/en/services/employment-law) and [Law of Obligations](/en/services/law-of-obligations).
