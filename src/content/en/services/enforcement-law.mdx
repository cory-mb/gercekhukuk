## Seeing the money, not just the claim

A valid claim remains only a claim until it is collected. Enforcement law governs collection through state power and is regulated by Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law No. 2004.

Deadlines in enforcement are short and formal rules strict. Choosing the wrong route can see months of proceedings halted by a single objection.

## For creditors

- **Enforcement based on a judgment:** Proceedings based on a court judgment or equivalent document.
- **Enforcement without a judgment:** The general attachment route for documented or undocumented claims.
- **Negotiable instruments:** A faster special route for cheques, promissory notes and bills of exchange.
- **Rent and eviction proceedings:** Payment deadline for unpaid rent followed by eviction.
- **Realisation of security:** Claims secured by mortgage or pledge.

### After commencement

- Actions to annul or lift objections
- Attachment of movables and immovables; garnishment notices to banks and third parties
- Sale and auction processes
- Ranking and third-party ownership disputes

Where the claim arises from a commercial or lease relationship, an action to annul an objection is subject to mandatory mediation, so a mediator must be approached before suing.

## For debtors

- Timely objection to the payment order
- Complaints against improper service, exempt assets and enforcement acts
- Negative declaratory and restitution actions
- Interim measures to stop attachment and sale
- Debt restructuring and settlement negotiations

## Bankruptcy and concordat

For businesses unable, or at risk of being unable, to pay debts as they fall due, concordat can allow operations to continue through an arrangement with creditors.

- Preparing a concordat application and following the project
- As creditor, assessing and objecting to a concordat proposal
- Bankruptcy applications and filing claims with the estate

## Before starting enforcement

- Gather documents proving the claim (contract, invoice, note, correspondence).
- Note what is known about the debtor's assets.
- Check whether the debtor has been put in default for interest purposes.
- Make sure the claim is not time-barred.
